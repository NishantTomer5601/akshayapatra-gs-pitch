# AIF Semifinal — 8-Minute Verbal Pitch · DRAFT V1
**Team:** Nishant Tomer · Pratham Goenka · Aadithya Muralidharan · Somya Sethi · Mohajit Neog
**Organisation:** The Akshaya Patra Foundation · **Grant:** $250,000

**Total: ~1,080 words ≈ 8:00 at a deliberate 135 wpm.** Each speaker ~215 words ≈ 1:30.
Delivery notes are in *[brackets]* and are **not spoken**.

---

## SPEAKER 1 — THE VESSEL *(1:30)*
### Owns: the myth, the origin, the scale. Job: make them care before they know anything.

Akshaya Patra means *"the vessel that never empties."*

*[Pause. Let it sit. Do not rush the next line.]*

It's from the Mahabharata. A pot, given to a family in exile, that fed everyone who came to it. But the part of the story people forget is the day it ran out. A sage arrived with a thousand disciples — and the pot was empty. So Krishna looked inside. And found one grain of rice, stuck to the rim.

That single grain fed all of them.

In the year 2000, a monk in Bengaluru borrowed that name for a kitchen that fed **fifteen hundred children.**

This March, the **President of India** served their **five billionth meal.**

Today, Akshaya Patra feeds **two point three five million children** — every school day, across twenty-five thousand government schools, in sixteen states. It is the largest school meal programme run by any NGO on earth.

And it runs on a promise so simple it's almost a dare: *no child in India shall be deprived of education because of hunger.*

Not *fed.* **Educated.** The meal was never the point. The meal is the thing that keeps a child in the room long enough to learn.

The five of us spent three months trying to understand how that promise gets kept two million times a day.

**So we went to Bengaluru. And we stood inside it.**

> **HANDOFF →** Speaker 2 opens on the kitchen floor. No greeting, no "thank you", no name. Just walk straight in.

---

## SPEAKER 2 — THE MACHINE *(1:30)*
### Owns: operational credibility. Job: make partners respect this as a business, not a charity.

**Kitchen 13 starts at four in the morning.**

By the time we got there, rice was already cooking — a thousand children's worth, every fifteen minutes. Beside it, a machine turning out **forty thousand rotis an hour.** The whole building is designed around gravity: ingredients go in at the top, cooked food comes out at the bottom, and no human hand touches it in between.

One roof. A hundred thousand children.

*[Beat. Shift tone — this is where it stops being a tour and starts being a system.]*

And then the part that changed how we thought about all of this.

The food doesn't leave in a truck. It leaves **against a clock.** Cooked rice has to be sealed into a steel vessel and be in front of a child while it is still above **sixty-five degrees.** Fourteen hundred vehicles. Seventy-kilometre routes. A **fifteen-minute window** at every school.

There is no warehouse in this supply chain. There is no inventory. Food is manufactured and consumed on the same morning — or it is garbage.

And there is **no buffer day.** Get tomorrow's plan wrong, and you don't fix it next week. A child eats less tomorrow.

We came in expecting to find a charity that needed money.

**We found a manufacturing operation running at world-class discipline — with two places where it is running completely blind.**

> **HANDOFF →** Speaker 3 picks up the word "blind" in their first sentence.

---

## SPEAKER 3 — THE BLIND SPOTS *(1:30)*
### Owns: the two gaps AND the reveal. **This is the most important 90 seconds in the pitch.**

**The first blind spot is at the beginning.**

Every night, someone decides what eighty kitchens will buy tomorrow. And they decide it the way it's been decided for twenty-five years — on experience, on a calendar, on a guess. Attendance shifts. Weather shifts. Prices shift. The guess doesn't.

Here is what that costs. In January, Akshaya Patra threw away **fifty-four thousand kilograms of vegetables.** Not carelessness — over-buying, because nobody could know. That is more than **one full day of lunch, for all two point three five million children.** Bought. Paid for. Trucked in. Never a meal.

**The second blind spot is at the end.** Fourteen hundred trucks on the road every morning — and not one of them can be seen. Do you know how Akshaya Patra finds out that lunch arrived? *The school calls them.* A teacher. With a phone.

*[Full stop. Change pace. Slow right down. This next passage is the drop.]*

But here is the thing we did not expect.

We pulled the capacity file for all eighty kitchens. **Akshaya Patra is not running out of kitchen.** There is room, today, for another **three hundred thousand meals a day.** The steel is built. The ovens are in. The staff are on payroll. The kitchen where all of this began is running at **thirty-six percent.**

**They are not short of capacity. They are short of certainty.**

So we asked a simple question: what does certainty cost?

> **HANDOFF →** Speaker 4's first words are the answer to that question. Answer it immediately — don't restate it.

---

## SPEAKER 4 — THE BUILD *(1:30)*
### Owns: the ask. Job: make $250k feel obvious, and make it clear this is *their* plan, not ours.

**Two hundred and fifty thousand dollars.**

And we didn't invent how to spend it. They'd already worked it out.

We asked their leadership directly: if Goldman Sachs handed you this money tomorrow, where does it do the most good? Everyone in this room expects them to say *"build us another kitchen."*

**They ranked that second.**

What they ranked first was the software.

So — **a hundred and fifty thousand** to close the first gap. One forecasting engine across all eighty kitchens, reading attendance, school calendars, menus and weather, and turning them into tomorrow's order. Buying each commodity when and where it's cheapest, instead of whenever it runs out. Wastage from five percent, down toward one.

**A hundred thousand** to close the second. GPS and door sensors across all fourteen hundred vehicles, geofenced to every school — so a delivery confirms *itself*, before the bell rings.

And on that one, we are not designing anything. There is a twelve-page document sitting on a server in Bengaluru that specifies the servers, the sensors, the database. It was written this July — **by an intern on their IT team.**

They have known exactly what to build for a year.

**They ran out of money before they ran out of ideas.**

So what does two hundred and fifty thousand dollars actually buy?

> **HANDOFF →** Speaker 5 answers with a number, immediately. No preamble.

---

## SPEAKER 5 — THE ARITHMETIC & THE CLOSE *(1:30)*
### Owns: the payoff and the landing. Job: make the maths feel inevitable, then get out clean.

**Seven cents.** That's what it costs to put a meal in front of a child in India. **Fifteen dollars and fifty-three cents** feeds one child for an entire school year.

So here is the whole case, in two numbers.

Spend two hundred and fifty thousand dollars on **food**, and you feed **sixteen thousand children** — for one year. Then it's gone.

Spend it on the **system**, and five percent comes off a forty-six-million-dollar food bill. That saving alone feeds **a hundred and fifty thousand children.** And there is already room for them — in kitchens we don't have to build.

And then next year, it happens again. And the year after that.

**The cheque is written once. The children are fed every year after.**

*[Beat. Drop your voice. The last forty seconds are quiet.]*

One last thing. There's a study in *Nature* that followed these children for twenty years. It found that a girl who ate a school lunch grows up to have taller, healthier children of her own.

The meal doesn't end when the bell rings. It doesn't even end with her.

The vessel in that story was never magic. On the day it mattered, the pot was empty — and someone simply looked closely enough to find the one grain that was already inside it.

**We've looked. It's there. And it costs two hundred and fifty thousand dollars to reach.**

Thank you.

# WHY IT'S BUILT THIS WAY

**The three engineered moments:**
1. **Open** — *"Akshaya Patra means the vessel that never empties."* Myth before metrics. They lean in before a single statistic arrives.
2. **Drop** — *"They are not short of capacity. They are short of certainty."* Speaker 3, ~4:15 in. Reframes the entire problem from money to information, and pays off everything before it.
3. **Close** — *"We've looked. It's there."* Closes the myth, converts a grant into due diligence — which is the one thing this audience does for a living.

**Total numbers spoken: 12.** Every one arrives as a consequence of the sentence before it. No number is ever announced; each one lands as the answer to something the audience is already wondering.

**Deliberately NOT said:** 655 vendors · 78 buyers / 145 approvers · 700 items · 200,000 vessels · $10.8M cumulative · the 2026–29 chart · 8.2% dropout · 102/123 GHI · ISO 22000 · ERP names. All Q&A-only. Speaking any of these breaks the spell.

**The handoff rule:** every speaker ends on an unresolved tension; the next speaker resolves it in their first sentence. Nobody summarises. Nobody says "and now my colleague will…". Nobody re-introduces the organisation. It should sound like one person thinking out loud in five voices.

**Casting note:** Speaker 3 carries the pitch. Give it to whoever is most comfortable slowing down and holding a silence. Speaker 5 needs the steadiest voice — the last four sentences must not be rushed.

**If you run long:** cut the *Nature* study from Speaker 5 (saves ~15s) and *"There is no warehouse… no inventory"* from Speaker 2 (~10s). Cut nothing from Speaker 3.
